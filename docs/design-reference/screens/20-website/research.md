# Website research: how the best builder-software sites read and earn trust

Read 2026-11-12 for Chris, in the built-in browser, light mode, desktop and phone, before refining the
Nov 4 site (`docs/reference/current-site/`). Eight sites, all selling software to home builders or
contractors. Layout, structure and copy habits only; nothing borrowed is a colour, a logo or a line of
their copy.

## The eight, one by one

**Buildertrend (buildertrend.com).** Hero: a dark photo with a strong overlay, an uppercase eyebrow
(#1 construction management software for contractors), a three-beat headline (Grow faster. Work
smarter. Build bigger.), one paragraph, one button (See pricing). Then: pick your builder type (home
builder, remodeler, commercial, trade); four outcome blocks each headed by a result (Win more of the
right jobs, Run every job without the chaos, Know your numbers before problems become expensive,
Clients stay informed. You stay focused.); three customer quotes with name and company; a stats band
that pairs an industry number with a customer number and names the source report under it;
onboarding reassurance (We understand adopting new tools can feel challenging); final call. Easy to
read because every section headline is an outcome, not a feature name. Trustworthy because every
stat has a named source and every quote has a person and a company. Worth borrowing: outcome-first
section headlines; the builder-type picker as a way to say who it's for; the onboarding paragraph
that admits switching is work. Avoid: 20,000+ builders and $10 million bands we can't back.

**JobTread (jobtread.com).** Hero: a product collage with a play button (JobTread in 8 minutes), a
plain headline, one orange button (Watch a full demo). Then: a review-badge row (5 stars on four
review sites, with counts); who it's for (eight trades, each with one line); four outcome sections,
each with a label, a headline and Learn more (Close more deals faster, Stay on time and on budget,
Increase your profits, Create happier customers); a long feature list grouped under those four
outcomes; integrations; a numbers band; one price. Easy to read because the four outcome groups
repeat all the way down, so the feature list has a home. Trustworthy because the review counts are
real and per site. Worth borrowing: the feature list grouped under outcomes (this is the Value
Equation offer stack almost exactly); an 8-minute demo video as the first proof. Avoid: eight
audience cards when we have one audience; the wall of 36 feature names.

**Houzz Pro (pro.houzz.com).** Hero: a full-bleed photo with a soft overlay, a short headline
(Build/Design the Future with AI, Today), two buttons, and an Ask anything box floating over the
photo. Then: tools by trade (carousel), three customer quotes with revenue numbers, five product
tabs, a 3-minute video, a 3 million+ line, the mobile app, final call. Easy to read because the type
is large and there is a lot of air. Trustworthy because the quotes carry a name, a company and a
specific number. Worth borrowing: the amount of white space; a quote band with real names (ours
stays empty until we have them). Avoid: the AI-first headline (says nothing to a builder); carousels
(hide content); a chat box in the hero.

**BuildBook (buildbook.co).** Hero: a photo of a builder on the phone on a framing site, a four-word
headline (Construction Management Software. Simplified.), one line (Fast. Powerful. Easy to use.
With everything you need at a fraction of the price.), one button, then a five-star quote from a
custom home builder right under the hero. Then: a one-line promise, a customer story (a couple with
a photo, their own words about family time), three tool groups, two audiences (custom home builders,
remodelers), a quote wall, Built by builders for builders, free templates, 340+ five-star reviews.
Easy to read because each section is one idea and one sentence; the type is big and the lines are
short. Trustworthy because the first proof is a named custom home builder and the story is about a
real family's time back. Worth borrowing: a quote from a custom builder directly under the hero (an
empty, labelled spot for now); the family-time outcome; two audiences at most. Avoid: Learn more on
every block; the HGTV name-drop.

**Buildxact (buildxact.com/us).** Hero: dark panel, left headline (Market leading AI you can trust),
one paragraph, two buttons (Get a tailored demo, Start your free trial, No credit card needed), a
video thumbnail at the right. Then: a company-you-can-trust band (companies, estimates a year,
dollars quoted, years, review score); logo wall; four outcome stats; one quote; four customer
stories with Watch the story; feature tabs; three plans explained in one paragraph each (I need to
estimate and bid, I need job management, I'm ready for it all); a closing line (Words are cheap.
Let's prove it to you in 15 minutes.). Easy to read because the plan section is written as the
buyer's own sentence. Trustworthy because the trust band is specific and the stories are named
companies. Worth borrowing: the plans written as what the buyer needs; a closing line that offers a
short, concrete next step with a time. Avoid: a cookie popup over the hero; AI in the headline.

**Bolster (bolsterbuilt.com).** Hero: a black page, yellow buttons, an email field in the hero (What's
your work email, Get started), a review-badge row, a vertical video with a founder talking. Then:
a stats row where each number names the customer it came from (5x estimating speed, Mesa Custom
Homes); a long, scrolling product walkthrough drawn as live UI (bid requests, compare bids, awarded
orders, bills matched, budget); three outcome tabs; an AI estimating story told as four steps with
real screens. Easy to read because the product is shown as working screens with one caption each.
Trustworthy because every number has a customer name beside it. Worth borrowing: product screens in
sequence with one caption each (our before-and-after pairs and three steps); numbers tied to a named
builder, never a bare percentage. Avoid: the email field in the hero; the dark page for a long read.

**Contractor Foreman (contractorforeman.com).** Hero: a centred headline (Easy, Affordable All-In-One
Construction Management Software), a three-fact band (Starts at $49/month, 100 Day Money Back
Guarantee, Easy to Use), a 4.5-star badge, two full-width buttons, a product collage. Then: a quote,
created by contractors, four audiences, six feature groups, integrations, a support section with
numbers (13-second average wait, 7am to 7pm ET, 95% five-star chats), final call, and a live chat
bubble. Easy to read because the three facts in the hero answer price, risk and ease at once.
Trustworthy because the guarantee and support hours are concrete and checkable. Worth borrowing: a
three-fact band under the hero (ours: [CONFIRM] price, [CONFIRM GUARANTEE], no app or password for
homeowners); support hours stated plainly. Avoid: the chat bubble; the busy collage.

**Buildern (buildern.com).** Hero: a plain headline, one paragraph, two buttons with a line under
each (7 days free, no credit card; Quick 20-min walkthrough), a logo wall. Then: an AI prompt box
with sample prompts; What kind of builder are you? with six photo cards and one line each; From
first estimate to final payment with four tabs; awards; what's new and a public roadmap; quotes with
names and titles; final call. Easy to read because every button says what happens next and how long
it takes. Trustworthy because the roadmap and release notes are public. Worth borrowing: a line
under each button saying what it costs in time; a public what's-new page later. Avoid: six audience
cards; the AI prompt box.

## What makes them easy to read

- Section headlines are outcomes, written as the builder would say them (Buildertrend, JobTread).
- One idea per section, one sentence of body, big type, lots of air (BuildBook, Houzz Pro).
- Product screens shown in sequence with one caption each, not described (Bolster).
- Buttons say what happens next and how long it takes (Buildern, Buildxact).
- Two audiences at most; the best small-builder site names one (BuildBook).

## What makes them trustworthy

- Every number sits next to a name (Bolster) or a named source (Buildertrend).
- Quotes carry a person, a company and a specific result (Houzz Pro, BuildBook).
- Price, guarantee and support hours stated plainly in one band (Contractor Foreman).
- Switching is acknowledged as work, with an onboarding promise (Buildertrend).
- A founder on camera or a founder's note (Bolster, and the Nov 4 site's A note from me).

## What's worth borrowing for the Nov 4 layout

1. Keep the Nov 4 video hero, left copy and the portal mock at the right, and make the headline the
   dream outcome (the three test headlines), one line on how, one button with a line under it saying
   what happens next.
2. A three-fact band under the hero: [CONFIRM] price, [CONFIRM GUARANTEE], homeowners need no app
   and no password.
3. Replace What it replaces with The cost of chasing: concrete costs, no invented statistics.
4. Before-and-after pairs and the three steps shown as real product screens with one caption each.
5. Features grouped under the outcome they deliver (the offer stack), never a flat list.
6. A quote band with empty, labelled spots until there are real beta builders to quote.
7. A line under every button; a closing line that repeats the dream outcome and names the next step.
8. No popups, no chat bubble, no carousel, no cookie banner over the hero.

## Note for Claude Code: no cookies

The site sets no cookies and loads nothing that does: no tracking pixel, no chat widget, no embedded
video player, no third-party fonts beyond Google Fonts. So there is no cookie banner, by design (Chris,
2026-11-12). If an analytics tool is ever added, it must be cookie-free (a server-side count or a
privacy-first script that sets no cookie and stores no identifier), so the page stays banner-free.
